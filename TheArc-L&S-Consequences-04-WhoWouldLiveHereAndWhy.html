1. Kenophobia (Fear of Empty Spaces)
  • Definition: Kenophobia is a specific phobia involving an intense, irrational fear or anxiety triggered by empty spaces, voids, vast open areas, or sparsely furnished environments. [1, 2]
  • Experience: In an empty city, the lack of human presence turns familiar boundaries and protective crowds into a vast, exposed void, which can trigger physical symptoms like dizziness, rapid heart rate, or panic. [1]
2. Kenopsia (The Eerie Void of Deserted Places)
  • Definition: Kenopsia is a term (popularized in modern lexicons like The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows) describing the uncanny, melancholic atmosphere of a place that is usually bustling with people—like a shopping mall, school hallway, or city street—but is now completely quiet and abandoned. [1]
  • Experience: It is not always active terror, but rather the haunting feeling of past life lingering in a space stripped of its purpose ("the ghost of human activity"). [1]
3. Agoraphobia
  • Definition: Agoraphobia is a clinical anxiety disorder where a person fears situations where escape might be difficult or help unavailable—often tied to open spaces, public areas, or crowds, though it can extend to feeling unsafe outside the home or in unfamiliar environments. [1, 2]
  • Context: Navigating large, deserted, or unfamiliar urban grids can activate this survival response because the absence of people removes a sense of safety or social scaffolding.
4. Ghost Cities and Real-World Examples
  • Physical Reality: Entire planned developments or urban districts—such as large-scale housing projects in parts of China or abandoned industrial towns like Chernobyl—stand as literal empty cities with modern skyscrapers, highways, and storefronts completely devoid of life. [1]
  • Psychological Reaction: Seeing architecture built explicitly for thousands of people sitting in total silence subverts human evolutionary expectations, as our brains rely on ambient social cues to signal that an environment is safe.
